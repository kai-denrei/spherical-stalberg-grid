# Rescue 2 — design (2026-09-05)

Operator's brief, verbatim: *"Rescue Game 2 now that we have the basics.
Larger map, more clearings. There are small groups of astronauts (with the
3D rendering, not the half-dotted one). They are hiding in containers,
while there are nearby large groups of rammable enemies. Once the Tank is
within shot distance of the astronaut, it triggers a walk animation that
gets the astronaut out of the container and into the tank. Saved. If the
tank is not static, they can be ran over and die. A little splash of red on
the floor."*

`?mission=rescue2`, beside `?mission=rescue` — **both stay**, so the two can
be played against each other. Decisions taken on the operator's behalf are
flagged **(taken)**.

## The shape of it

Rescue 1 is a defence with a ferry. Rescue 2 is a **raid**: the map is
static, the threat is pre-placed, and every camp is a small problem you
solve in the same three beats.

1. **Drive in.** A camp is a container in a clearing with a large garrison
   of rammable enemies around it. Ramming is free and unlimited — that is
   what the garrison is for.
2. **Call them out.** Inside *shot distance* of the container it opens, and
   the group walks out toward the tank, one at a time.
3. **Stand still.** A walking astronaut that the moving hull touches is run
   over: dead, and a splash of red stays on the floor for the rest of the
   run. Reaching the tank is a save, on the spot — there is no ferry home.

**(taken) No gate waves.** The gates stand as scenery and as targets, but
nothing spawns. The brief says "more tactical" and names the threat as
*pre-placed groups*; a wave clock on top of that turns a puzzle back into a
defence, which is the mission we already have.

**(taken) Saved on boarding, not on delivery.** The brief ends the sentence
at "into the tank. Saved." — so there is no run home and no seat limit.

## The map

**(taken)** `points 1600`, `rooms 26`, `roomRadius 5`, `obstacles 0.16` —
larger, and the room count is what "more clearings" means on this
generator. The **whole shell is open from the first frame**: sector gating
is a campaign progression and a raid has nowhere to progress to.

## The camps

`camps: 3`, each: one container, `2..3` astronauts inside, a garrison of
`garrison: 10` rammable units ringed around it at `garrisonCells: 2.5`,
plus `hard: 1` solid unit from the second camp onward. Camps sit in room
centres, `campApart: 12` cells from each other and from the heart, and the
placer degrades that spacing before it degrades the count — the rule Rescue
1 already learned.

The garrison is **asleep** until the tank comes within `wakeCells: 6`, then
walks the tank down like any other hostile. **(taken)** A camp you have not
reached should not be walking across the map at you; that is what makes
them camps and not a wave.

## The extraction

- `callCells: 4.0` — *shot distance*. Inside it, the container opens
  (a sound, a ring, the doors) and the group emerges on a `emergeGap: 0.9 s`
  stagger.
- A walker moves toward the tank's **current** position along the great
  circle at `walkSpeed: 1.1` cells/s. It re-aims every frame, so a tank that
  repositions is followed rather than walked past.
- Within `boardCells: 0.9` of the hull → **saved**, on the spot.
- **Run over:** the hull within `boardCells` while moving faster than
  `runoverSpeed: 0.6` cells/s kills the walker. Dead, and a splash of red
  dots stays on the ground **for the rest of the run** — **(taken)** it does
  not fade, because it is the record of a mistake and a mission about
  carrying people out should keep score of the ones it did not.
- A hostile touching a walker grabs it, on Rescue 1's window and code
  (`stepGrab`, 1.5 s, freed if you kill the grabber).
- An astronaut still inside a container cannot be hurt at all.

## The astronauts are the GLB

The brief is explicit: *"with the 3D rendering, not the half-dotted one."*
`assets/models/astronaut.glb`, the study's own file.

The cost objection raised when Rescue 1 chose dot clouds does not apply
here, and the reason is worth writing down: it was about six separate
**loads** of a 3.7 MB file. One parse plus N clones that **share the
geometry and the materials** is one upload and nine GPU-skinned meshes of
25 bones — nothing. What it needs is a correct clone.

**(taken) The clone is written here, not vendored.** `SkeletonUtils` exists
in four sibling repos' `node_modules`, all of them three r180; this project
vendors r160, and "an addon from the sibling's node_modules" is already a
recorded dead end in `.deban`. `cloneSkinned()` in `units.js` is twenty
lines: clone the graph, pair the bones by traversal order, and re-`bind`
each SkinnedMesh to a new Skeleton over the cloned bones. Without the
rebind every clone shares one skeleton and they all deform — and stand — as
one body.

## 1. The module — `src/rescue.js` (extended)

```
RESCUE2_TUNE = {
  camps: 3, groupMin: 2, groupMax: 3,
  garrison: 10, hard: 1, garrisonCells: 2.5, wakeCells: 6,
  campApart: 12, heartApart: 10,
  callCells: 4.0, emergeGap: 0.9,
  walkSpeed: 1.1, boardCells: 0.9, runoverSpeed: 0.6,
  grabSecs: 1.5,
}
```

- `makeCamps(st, cands, rng, cellSide, tune)` — camps in clearings, spaced,
  each with its group; degrades `campApart` before the count.
- `stepCall(camp, tankPos, cellSide, tune)` → `'open'` on the frame it opens.
- `stepEmerge(camp, dt, tune)` → the next survivor to step out, or null.
- `walkStep(sv, tankPos, dt, cellSide, tune)` → the new position, and
  `'saved'` when it arrives.
- `runOver(sv, tankPos, tankSpeed, cellSide, tune)` → boolean.
- `awake(camp, tankPos, cellSide, tune)` → is this garrison up.

Tests: camps are spaced and deterministic and degrade before shrinking; a
container outside `callCells` does not open and inside it opens once; a
walker closes the distance every step and arrives; a walker arrives at a
tank that has MOVED since it set off; nothing is run over by a stationary
hull and everything is by a moving one at the same distance; a walker
inside a container is immune; the whole group emerges on the stagger.

## 2. The board

Containers (`makeContainerFixture`), the GLB astronauts, the garrisons, the
splash decals, the HUD row, the end card, and `?rescue2probe=1`.

## Out of scope (named)

A carry/climb-in animation (the walker vanishes into the hull with a burst);
astronauts that shoot back; a container you can shoot open; camps that
respawn; the mission on a menu card rather than a URL.
